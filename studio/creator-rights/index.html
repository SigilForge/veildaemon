License guidance

Not sure which license fits your project?

Answer a few plain-language questions and the picker explains your options, recommends an appropriate SPDX license or custom terms, and shows what alternatives would change. That Advisor guidance is useful even if you never publish a Creator Rights Record.

Ask creators' questions

The picker starts with goals creators recognize: commercial use, attribution, forks, open modifications, reciprocal sharing, AI training, and when separate permission should be required.

Explain tradeoffs

A recommendation can say why MPL, MIT, Apache, AGPL, Creative Commons, proprietary, or custom terms fit better, and what a different choice would change downstream.

Keep the creator in control

The software can guide, compare, and prefill. The creator still reviews the evidence, chooses the final license, and decides whether to publish the record.

Artifact first

Give it the work. Review what it found.

Creator Rights is designed for working creators, not licensing analysts. The public front door is a source or file, and the detailed form becomes the advanced editor after import.

Provide a source

One source field accepts supported URLs or identifiers. Current external sources include GitHub, npm, PyPI, DOI/Crossref, ISBN/Open Library, Steam, and itch.io.

Upload a file

Local file import supports EPUB, PDF and Office documents, Blender and 3D files, Unity assets, images, audio, video, datasets, source files, and LICENSE-like files.

Reuse the fingerprint

Uploaded files are fingerprinted with SHA-256 in the browser. The same uploaded file and calculated hash are reused later in the workflow; there is no second required upload.

Draft evidence

Imported values are reviewed, not worshipped.

Artifact first. Draft, not fact. Agreement increases confidence. Disagreement lowers confidence. Conflict never resolves silently.

Evidence is labeled

Imported fields keep their source, confidence, review status, and candidate values so the creator can see what was found and what still needs judgment.

Agreement helps

When sources match, the draft can mark that agreement and raise confidence without pretending the machine made a legal determination.

Conflict requires review

If license sources disagree, every candidate is preserved and displayed. No source wins silently; the creator must choose the governing license or mark the record as custom.

License composition

Copyright license first. SFR alongside it.

Assistive picker

The license picker asks plain-language questions about the creator's goals, narrows appropriate SPDX options or recommends custom terms when needed, and explains why each license is suggested. It is not an automatic legal decision.

Creator decides

Imported repository or package metadata may prefill or suggest a license, but the creator can override the draft after review. When evidence conflicts, the picker enters a review state instead of silently selecting a winner.

Traceable recommendation

Every recommendation should be explainable and traceable to the creator's answers, the selected work type, imported evidence, or preserved conflict candidates.

Primary license

The selected SPDX or copyright license remains the work's license: MIT, Apache, MPL, Creative Commons, proprietary, or another supported option.

SFR framework

The SigilForge Rights Framework layers provenance, verification state, AI permissions, evidence semantics, and machine-readable rights metadata on top of the chosen license. It does not replace or modify that license.

Publication verification FAQ

Questions creators already ask.

Is this a subscription?

No. Each Creator Rights Record is covered by a one-time $1.99 Founders lifetime Registry license. Your purchase includes permanent hosting of the record, public verification, Creator Dossier generation, and future access to that record. There are no recurring fees required to keep an existing record active.

Do I always control my records?

Yes. Every Creator Rights Record can be exported at any time as a self-contained Creator Dossier, including JSON metadata, evidence manifests, SHA-256 fingerprints, and public rights declarations. Your evidence remains under your control regardless of platform hosting.

Does a Creator Rights Record replace copyright registration?

No. Creator Rights Records complement existing legal, licensing, and publishing systems. Depending on your jurisdiction and goals, you may also wish to register copyright with official authorities (such as the U.S. Copyright Office), obtain an ISBN or DOI, or publish through established distribution platforms.

Does a record prove legal ownership?

No. It documents a declared rights position, publication metadata, version history, and evidence labels. Legal ownership can depend on facts and law beyond this record.

How do I prove I published first?

A public record can document the declared publication date, recorded version, rights holder, and later version history. It is provenance evidence, not a court ruling.

How do I tell AI systems not to train on my content?

The record publishes AI training permissions, licensing requirements, and machine-readable metadata so crawlers, licensing teams, and reviewers can read the creator's declared policy.

Can I record books, games, artwork, PDFs, or software releases?

Yes, when you have authority to create the record. The intake accepts supported URLs and identifiers, plus local files such as EPUB, PDF, Office documents, Blender and 3D files, Unity assets, images, audio, video, datasets, source files, and LICENSE-like files.

Does imported metadata verify ownership?

No. Imported values create a draft, not a verified rights claim. GitHub ownership, package ownership, usernames, and publication metadata are evidence to review; they are not proof of authorship or rights ownership.

What are AI permissions?

AI permissions describe the creator's declared position for training, fine-tuning, embeddings, retrieval, generation, dataset redistribution, research use, and commercial AI licensing.

What if AI scraped my website or work?

A record cannot undo scraping, but it can publish a clear creator rights declaration, AI usage policy, licensing route, and timestamped publication metadata for future reference.

Why would this matter years later?

If questions arise regarding publication history, licensing, AI permissions, or provenance, the Creator Rights Record preserves a contemporaneous record of what was declared, what evidence supported those declarations, and how the record evolved over time. It does not guarantee legal outcomes.

Do I need an account to begin?

No. The Creator Rights Advisor is free and public. An account is only needed when you choose to preserve or publish a permanent Registry record and manage it over time.

Why include a SHA-256 hash?

A SHA-256 hash can connect a specific file fingerprint to the publication record. Uploaded files are fingerprinted in the browser and the same calculated hash is reused later in the workflow. It does not prove authorship by itself.

Is SFR the copyright license?

No. The selected SPDX or copyright license remains separate. The SigilForge Rights Framework adds provenance, verification state, AI permissions, evidence semantics, and machine-readable rights metadata. It does not replace or modify the selected license.

Does the license picker make the legal decision for me?

No. The picker is assistive. It asks plain-language questions, narrows likely SPDX licenses or recommends custom terms, explains the suggestion, and leaves the final selection to the creator.

What does the QR link do?

The QR code points to the permanent public record URL so readers, licensors, crawlers, and archives can find the current publication record and machine-readable metadata.
